Key concept Anti-pattern

Gated Knowledge

The anti-pattern: understanding locked behind a person, a licence or a login — access controlled by whoever holds the key rather than by whether you need it.

Understanding exists, but you have to get past someone to reach it. A person who must be asked. A licence that must be bought. A tool whose model is only legible inside the tool. A login that a contractor will not be given.

The knowledge is not missing — that would be Tribal Knowledge. It is written down, structured, often excellent. It simply sits behind a gate, and the gate is not there because the knowledge is sensitive. It is there because of how the system happened to be built, or because someone benefits from the gate.

The three gates

The person gate. "Ask Marieke." Marieke is helpful and knows the answer, so nobody fixes it — the gate works well enough to survive for years, and only fails when Marieke leaves.

The tool gate. The model lives in a modelling tool; the lineage lives in a governance platform; the definitions live in a BI semantic layer. Each is readable — if you have a seat. Understanding your own organisation now costs a licence per reader, which quietly decides who is allowed to understand it.

The format gate. Technically open, practically closed: a 400-page PDF, a diagram exported as a flat image, a proprietary file no other system reads. Nothing is hidden, and nothing is usable either.

Why it is worse now

A gate that a person can climb, an AI cannot. A model locked in a vendor's format is invisible to an agent reasoning over your architecture — so the very system meant to make knowledge accessible becomes the reason it is unreachable. The gate that used to slow people down now stops the machine entirely, which is a strong argument for the plain-text, model-first stance: markdown, YAML, open schemas, generated diagrams.

There is also an uncomfortable version worth naming. Gates sometimes exist *on purpose* — a consultant who stays essential, a vendor whose value is that you cannot leave, a team whose influence depends on being asked. Those gates are defended with arguments about governance and quality. The test is simple: does opening it cost anyone their position?

The opposite

Not "everything public" — that is a different failure. The opposite is knowledge that travels with the work: definitions in the model, decisions in decision records, structure in files anyone with a text editor can open, diagrams generated from the source rather than drawn beside it. Access decided by need, not by who holds a key.
