Anti-pattern

Model As Drawing

The anti-pattern: treating the model as a picture to be drawn rather than knowledge to be captured — so it informs nobody and generates nothing.

The model is treated as a picture. Boxes and lines, arranged until they look right, exported to PNG, pasted into a deck. It may be accurate. It is not *usable*: nothing is generated from it, nothing is validated against it, and the reasoning behind every choice lives outside it.

The giveaway is what happens when reality changes. A drawing must be redrawn — by hand, by whoever owns the file, if they remember. A model gets updated once and everything downstream follows.

Modelling is knowledge engineering, not illustration

A data model is an organisation's structured knowledge about itself: what a customer is, when an order becomes an order, which facts are allowed to coexist. Capturing that is making the implicit explicit — closer to an interview than to drawing.

Treated as illustration, the hard part is skipped. You can draw a plausible entity called `Customer` without ever discovering that three departments define it three different ways. The picture looks finished precisely because the disagreement was never surfaced.

What it costs

- No generation. DDL, pipelines, tests and documentation are written by hand, next to a diagram that claims to describe them. They drift, and the diagram is blamed for being wrong. - No provenance. An entity exists; why it exists, who agreed it and which interview produced it are nowhere. The next modeller re-derives it or, more often, overrides it. - No validation. Nothing checks whether the data honours the model, so the model becomes an aspiration. - Gates. Drawings live in tools (Gated Knowledge) and get shared as flat images — readable by people with a licence, invisible to everything else. - Unreadable to AI. A PNG of a model carries none of its semantics. The moment you want an agent to reason over your architecture, a drawn model contributes nothing.

The distinction that matters

Diagrams are not the problem — hand-maintained diagrams are. A diagram generated from the model is one of the most useful artifacts there is: always current, cheap to regenerate, and different views for different audiences (Living Diagrams). The rule is directional: read the picture from the source; never transcribe the source into a picture.

Sketching by hand *during* modelling is also fine, and often essential — that is thinking, and thinking on a shared canvas is how agreement happens. The anti-pattern is when the sketch becomes the deliverable.

The test

What does this model produce? If the honest answer is "a picture in a document", it is a drawing. If it produces schemas, tests, documentation, diagrams and answers to questions about provenance, it is a model.
