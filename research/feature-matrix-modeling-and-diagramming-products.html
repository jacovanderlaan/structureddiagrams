# Feature matrix — modelling products vs diagramming tools

Question: which features do data modelling products and generic diagramming tools offer, how does each score, and which should we build ourselves?

The finding that organises the rest: the two categories fail in opposite directions. Modelling products have the model but treat the picture as an export. Diagramming tools have the picture but no model underneath. Almost nothing sits in the middle — which is where the buildable list comes from.

⚠️ Read this as a working assessment, not a benchmark. Scores are judgements from documentation, hands-on use and the research already in this folder, dated 31 Jul 2026. Vendors move; verify before quoting.

PowerDesigner is still the most complete on impact analysis, and it treats an impact model as a first-class saved artifact rather than a transient report — worth stealing. ⚠️ End of life 2027. Decades of enterprise models are locked in a proprietary format that will outlive its tool: Gated Knowledge with a closing date.

Ellie.ai wins on the axis everyone else ignores: real-time browser collaboration with business users, an AI chatbot for first drafts, catalog integration. It is genuinely better at this than we will be. The trade is ownership — the model lives in their cloud.

dbdiagram scores unexpectedly well on the bottom four rows for one reason: its source is text. Small tool, correct instinct.

draw.io is the best all-rounder for our purposes: open format, generatable, parseable, custom libraries, clickable output, free. yEd owns exactly one row and owns it completely — the 200-node graph nobody can hand-arrange. Mermaid wins the row that matters most in practice: it renders where the code already lives.

Excalidraw's sketchy look is a genuine feature, not a limitation — it signals provisional and invites correction where a crisp render says decided.

3 · The gap between the two tables

Read them together and the shape is obvious:

Nothing occupies the middle. A modelling product with an open source format and multi-notation generated output does not exist commercially — which is precisely the position this work already holds, arrived at from the metadata side rather than the tooling side.

The pattern in rows 1–8: every one is packaging work over generators that already exist. None requires inventing anything. That is what makes vibe-coding viable here — the specification is the research, and the research is done.

The pattern in rows 9–10: both are products in their own right, both have well-funded incumbents, and neither strengthens the actual thesis.

5 · Conclusions

1. The categories fail in opposite directions. Model without picture; picture without model. The middle is unoccupied.

2. Open format is the dividing line — again. Every tool that scores well on survives its vendor has a text or documented format.

3. PowerDesigner's 2027 EOL is a dated opportunity, not just a fact. Reverse-engineering .pdm has a shelf life.

4. Ellie.ai is better at collaboration and will stay better. Compete on ownership and generation, not on real-time UX.

5. Eight features are buildable as packaging work. The generators exist; what is missing is the wrapper.

6. Two features should be declined outright — say so explicitly, so they stop reappearing in planning.
